01 · Context

From expert experience to an institutional system

Traffic Light designs, installs and maintains public lighting, traffic signals and urban infrastructure for municipalities and private clients, with operations aligned to ISO 9001. Its operations area wanted training and field audits that scale with the business.

Turn the know-how of experienced technicians into a structured, measurable system that any trainer can run and every crew benefits from.

SMART objective

Develop an integrated audit and training system for the operations area using Soft Systems Methodology, moving from a reactive model to a closed-loop, continuous-improvement model by the end of the 2026 academic period.

03 · Diagnosis

The missing piece was a feedback loop

The rich picture pointed to one systemic idea: without a feedback loop, field errors tend to repeat; with one, every audit feeds training and the system corrects itself.

Without a feedback loop

Linear and reactive: problems are fixed, not prevented.

Operational excellence
Quality · safety
competitive in tenders

With the proposed system

Preventive: data triggers audits, audits drive training.

Clients: government & privateTenders: budget & deadlinesLarge field crewsAsset & incident dataOnboarding new staffContinuous improvement

Relationship diagram

For each pair of factors we asked "does this one cause that one?" and counted causal arrows. The factor with only outgoing arrows was a structured method to teach: it unlocks the matrix, the checklists and the audits. Effectiveness KPIs only receive arrows, so they measure the result.

Design requirements we derived

  • A unique ID per inspection, for traceability
  • Required fields: auditor, crew, location, date and time
  • Photo evidence before, during and after each job
  • Each nonconformity as its own record, with owner and deadline
  • Audits triggered by operations data, not only perception
04 · Solution

Three alternatives, one integrated system

ALTERNATIVE A

Skills matrix only

Shows who needs training and in what, but gives no method to close the gaps.

Diagnosis without treatment
ALTERNATIVE B

Training manual only

Standardizes how to teach, but risks training people who already know and skipping those who don't.

Treatment without diagnosis
SELECTED
ALTERNATIVE C

Matrix + manual as one loop

The matrix answers "whom and what to train"; the manual answers "how to train well". Checklists feed results back into the matrix.

Knowledge becomes institutional

Training needs by competency

What the supervisor reads from it

    1 · Not cleared

    Does not know the procedure or has never done the task alone.

    → Urgent training. No field work without full supervision.

    2 · In progress

    Knows the theory and can do the task, but needs supervision to ensure quality and avoid rework.

    → Guided practice: 10–20 min sessions on site.

    3 · Expert

    Masters the task and works autonomously and safely.

    → Internal instructor for level 1 and 2 peers.
    06 · Training manual

    A 50+ page program and trainer's guide

    Every session follows the same five steps, so any qualified trainer delivers the same content to the same standard.

    1

    Explain

    The goal and why the skill matters, with real field examples.

    2

    Demonstrate

    The trainer does the task, pointing out critical points and common errors.

    3

    Practice together

    Participants try it while the trainer guides and corrects on the spot.

    4

    Evaluate

    Each person does it alone and is rated with the module checklist.

    5

    Feedback

    Strengths, areas to improve and extra practice if needed.

    Technical modules

    Select a module to see its units and suggested hours.

    Evaluation

    Diagnostic, practical and final evaluations with a three-level checklist: does it correctly alone, does it with help, does not do it. Trainees rate each session in an anonymous survey.

    Standards & safety

    NOM-001-SEDE-2012NOM-029-STPS-2011NOM-017-STPS-2008NOM-031-STPS-2011NOM-030-ENER-2012PPE protocols

    Beyond the modules

    Strategic courses (design thinking, project management, continuous improvement), three field case studies, a glossary, practice logs and a post-training follow-up plan.

    How success is measured · 11 KPIs

    Audit system

    1. Scheduled audits completed
    2. Nonconformities by type of work
    3. Time to close nonconformities
    4. Audits triggered by data

    Training system

    1. Matrix coverage of staff
    2. Competency gap
    3. Training plan compliance
    4. Effectiveness in the field

    Operational impact

    1. Rework reduction
    2. First-review contract compliance
    3. Incidents reported from operations
    Change resistance

    Present evaluations as development and involve staff in validating the tools.

    Trainer workload

    Start with micro-trainings and grow internal instructors fast.

    Data quality

    Train checklist users, spot-check records, move formats to digital.

    Staff turnover

    Build evaluation and training into onboarding; the manual keeps the knowledge.
