01 · Context

Electricity is the biggest variable cost of an arc furnace

An electric arc furnace melts scrap and direct-reduced iron (DRI) with an electric arc between graphite electrodes. Every heat uses a slightly different charge, recipe and operating time, so energy per ton varies from heat to heat. Ternium asked us to understand that variation with data.

The question: which variables really drive energy per ton, and can we predict it before the heat starts?

Data

Historical heat records with dozens of variables per heat: charge mix, chemistry inputs, oxygen and carbon injection, power, delays and steel grade.

Response variable

Electric energy per ton of liquid steel (kWh/t), with tapping temperature as a quality constraint that any recommendation must respect.

03 · Statistical analysis

Proving the causes before modeling them

We prioritized causes by impact on energy and by whether data could test them, then chose a statistical test for each type of relationship.

Which test for which question

  • Pearson & Spearman: linear and monotonic relationships between each input and energy, to spot variables that have an optimum rather than a straight line.
  • ANOVA: whether energy differs between steel grade families, which would justify grade-specific recipes.
  • Chi-square: whether some grades are linked to missing the target tapping temperature.
  • Shapiro-Wilk: whether energy is close to normal, a requirement before using parametric SPC charts.

Variables, classified

The columns were sorted by what an operator can act on. Outliers were kept and reported: in steelmaking they are real heats, not errors.

08 · Improve & control

From a model to daily decisions

Quick wins

  • Track kWh/t per heat against a target
  • Attack the most frequent delay causes
  • Standardize key settings across shifts
  • Review lime use per ton
  • Balance oxygen and carbon injection

Operational

  • Adjust the charge recipe (DRI–scrap mix) to the material available

Analytical

  • Predict expected energy before each heat
  • Alert when a heat is at risk of high consumption

Strategic

  • Grow the model into a digital twin of the furnace
  • Run virtual DOE to test settings without risk
